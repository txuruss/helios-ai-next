# Helios AI Client Acquisition Doctrine

## Summary

The core operating belief for Helios AI: **Client first. Problem second. Tool third. Build last.** Most beginners learn tools (n8n, Vapi, Make, Relevance AI, Claude Code) and then force them onto a business. Helios works the opposite way — find a real business, find a real bottleneck, confirm they care, sell the audit/meeting/outcome, then build only what solves the problem.

## Status

`In progress` — living doctrine. This is the lens for every acquisition decision.

## The rule

> Client first. Problem second. Tool third. Build last.

Work order:

1. Find a real business.
2. Find a real bottleneck.
3. Confirm the business cares about solving it.
4. Sell the audit, meeting, or outcome.
5. Build only what solves the problem.

## Core principle

**Tools without clients are a hobby.**

Do not keep building features unless they help with one of these:

- Finding leads
- Qualifying businesses
- Booking calls
- Closing clients
- Delivering the AI booking system
- Improving client results
- Retaining clients

If a piece of work doesn't move one of those, it is a distraction. See [[Helios AI Founder Operating Rules]] (Rule 1: do not hide behind building).

## The 3 Cs

| C | Meaning |
|---|---------|
| **Capabilities** | Understand what AI can actually do for a real business — how it fits inside booking, lead capture, FAQs, follow-ups, alerts, dashboards, and workflows. |
| **Contact** | Get in front of real owners — warm outreach, in-person, cold email, Instagram DMs, and simple content. |
| **Convert** | Don't pitch immediately. Ask questions, identify bottlenecks, explain outcomes, show how the system captures more appointments. |

## Action items

- [ ] Every build task must trace to one of the seven acquisition activities above.
- [ ] Default to Contact + Convert when unsure what to work on.
