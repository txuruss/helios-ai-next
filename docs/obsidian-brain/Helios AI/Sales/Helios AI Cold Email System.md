# Helios AI Cold Email System

## Summary

Cold email is **not a one-time message — it is a cycle.** It works when Helios has an offer people demonstrably want, a defined ICP, a clear value proposition, and a willingness to test and optimize. It fails for predictable reasons: the email isn't seen, the message is wrong, outreach stops too early, or the outreach is based on assumptions.

## Status

`In progress` — living playbook. Pairs with [[Helios AI Outreach Infrastructure]] (deliverability) and [[Outreach Strategy]] (channel mix).

## The cycle

1. Set up infrastructure → see [[Helios AI Outreach Infrastructure]]
2. Research the ICP
3. Build the campaign
4. Launch the campaign
5. Review campaign data
6. Iterate

## Cold email works only when Helios has

- An offer people demonstrably want
- A clearly defined ICP
- A clear value proposition
- A willingness to test and optimize consistently

## Why cold email works

- **Scalable** — reach more businesses once the system works
- **Measurable** — every part can be tracked and improved
- **Controlled** — not dependent on social algorithms
- **Precise** — target exact business types
- **Cost-effective** — generate leads without paid ads

## Why cold email fails

**1. The email is not being seen.** Sending from the main domain with no setup → spam/rejected before a human sees it. (No separate outreach domain, no warmup, no SPF/DKIM/DMARC, poor sender reputation.) Fix this in [[Helios AI Outreach Infrastructure]].

**2. The message is wrong.**
- Weak: *"We build AI automation systems. Want to hop on a call?"* — asks for trust before the prospect has a reason to care.
- Better: *"Would it be useful if I sent you a 2-minute video showing where your salon may be losing booking inquiries?"* — sells **curiosity**, not the service.

**3. Outreach stops too early.** Sending 20–50 messages, getting no replies, and concluding the offer fails is weak testing. Cold outreach works through **volume, follow-up, and iteration** — treat it as a sequence, not a single message.

**4. The outreach is based on assumptions.** Generic pitches lose. Before messaging, research:
- What they sell
- How customers book
- Whether they use WhatsApp, forms, phone calls, DMs, or website chat
- Whether responses are slow
- Reviews mentioning booking, communication, waiting, or missed calls
- Weak website CTAs
- Instagram with unanswered questions or booking friction

## Messaging rules — sell the meeting, not the service

The first message should **not** try to close. It creates curiosity and starts a conversation.

- Bad: *"We install AI booking systems for local businesses. Do you want to schedule a call?"*
- Better: *"I noticed your salon takes bookings through Instagram and WhatsApp. Would it be useful if I sent over a quick 2-minute breakdown showing where you may be losing booking inquiries before they become appointments?"*

### First message formula

```
Hey [Name], I noticed [specific observation about their business].

A lot of [business type] lose bookings when inquiries come through [channel]
but do not get answered fast enough.

Would it be useful if I sent you a quick 2-minute video showing where your
booking flow may be leaking leads?
```

### Subject line examples

Use specific, curiosity-based lines. **Avoid:** "AI Automation Solutions for [Company Name]".

- Quick idea for [Business Name]
- Possible missed bookings at [Business Name]
- Noticed this on your booking flow
- Question about your WhatsApp bookings
- 2-minute idea for [Business Name]
